Design brief · a proposal, not a decision
Atlas

Renaming the MAP. One common word, no article, and a script wordmark that makes it the third mark in the family — beside Continuing Education and the Clearing.

The case

An atlas is a book of maps — and that is what this site has quietly become. The connections graph, the city map, the genre staffs, the era bars, the moments wire, the scene spine: no single map, a bound collection of them. "MAP" names one view; "Atlas" names the volume.

The word carries the right ancestry. Mercator coined it in 1595 for a bound collection of maps, after the titan who holds the heavens; Aby Warburg's Mnemosyne Atlas made it the name for a scholarly atlas of cultural memory — images arranged by association and influence rather than geography. That is nearly this site's mission statement.

And article-less, as proposed, it stops being an object and becomes a name. "Atlas holds the lines of influence." It pairs with the Listener — the site's two personae, one that holds and one that hears.

All three set in the same roundhand. Continuing Education's mark is already drawn to SVG outlines; the other two here are your device's Snell Roundhand rendering live — which is also the production honest-truth: Snell is an Apple system font, not embeddable as a webfont, so the chosen wordmark ships as drawn paths exactly the way the CE mark does. Visitors then see the real thing everywhere.

The one real collision. The NYC map is currently called "the Atlas." Under the new name it simply becomes what it already is — Places: the panel chip reads PLACES, the toolbar icon's label follows, and the standalone /atlas/ page (already superseded by the PLACES landing) retires. The site-level name takes the word back for the whole.

  • Wordmarks — toolbar (home + rail), About card masthead, loader landing, og site name, browser titles.
  • Copy — the July 20 sweep catalogued every live string; "the MAP" appears in a known, finite set of lines. A day of careful work, not a month.
  • The app — display name and TestFlight listing.
  • URLs stay put — /map/ keeps working; the name is identity, not routing. A redirect project can come later or never.
  • Internal docs — MAPmaking, Using the MAP, the gears: rename or keep as working titles; nothing user-facing forces it.
  • Timing — the Constitution and Manifesto aren't ratified yet. Deciding before they are is the cheapest this rename will ever be.

To decide
